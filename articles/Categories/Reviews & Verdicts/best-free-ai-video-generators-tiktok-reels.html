5 Best Free AI Video Generators for TikTok & Reels in 2026 (I Tested 12)

30-Second Quick Take

Start with CapCut for editing and captions, and add Kling AI for free cinematic clips. Pika is the best scroll-stopping hook, Canva is the easiest for beginners, and InVideo AI only works free if you can live with a watermark. Heads up: free AI credits are small, and several free plans add a logo or block commercial use, so read the gotchas section before you monetise anything.

Tested hands-on over 2 weeks, 12 tools tried • Plan limits checked against official pricing pages and published reviews • Last checked: October 2026

In the last 2 weeks I tested 12 free AI video generators to make vertical videos for TikTok and Reels. Most promise “free” but slap a huge watermark on the export or only give you 3 seconds of footage.

These 5 actually worked and exported 9:16 without paying. Some still leave a small logo, and I'll tell you exactly which ones.

How I Picked These 5

Out of the 12 tools, a tool only made the list if it passed four checks:

  • It exports without paying. A free trial that locks the export button didn't count.
  • It outputs vertical 9:16. I need video for TikTok and Reels, not widescreen that I have to crop.
  • It is honest about watermarks. Tools can still leave a logo, but I flag it in the review so you know before you start.
  • The clips are usable. Three seconds of blurry footage is not a video, so tools with unusable output were cut.

1. CapCut: Best Overall (And Actually Free)

Official site: capcut.com

If I could only install one, it would be this. CapCut is not just an editor anymore. Its AI script-to-video feature can turn a prompt like “Make a TikTok about 3 AI tools for students” into a finished vertical video with auto-captions, B-roll and voiceover.

  • Free plan: Unlimited exports for normal editing. In my tests there was no watermark once I deleted the last 2-second CapCut outro clip.
  • Best for: Faceless TikToks, captions, templates
  • Limitation: You need to download the app. It isn't fully browser-based.

I made 3 TikToks in 20 minutes with it. The auto-caption accuracy was around 95%, even with my accent.

Research note (not tested by me): CapCut's free plan covers manual editing at up to 1080p with no watermark on standard exports. Published reviews say a watermark can appear if your project keeps a Pro-locked template, or if you export certain AI-generated clips, and that the AI credits on the free plan are small. Check the export preview before you post.

2. Pika: Best for Stylish AI Clips

Official site: pika.art

Pika is different. I typed a prompt like “a sneaker floating in neon Tokyo, vertical video” and it generated a 4-second stylish clip. It's perfect as the hook for a TikTok.

  • Free plan: About 80 credits a month, refreshing monthly. Free output is capped at 480p and carries a Pika watermark, according to its pricing page and recent reviews. That is roughly 3–6 short clips, which is enough for hooks but not for a whole TikTok.
  • Best for: Viral hooks, product shots, anime-style clips
  • Limitation: Short clips only (3–4 seconds), so you need to stitch them together in CapCut.

Tip: Use Pika for the first 3 seconds to stop the scroll, then cut to CapCut. Because credits go fast, plan your prompt before you generate, since failed or repeated generations still use credits.

3. Canva Magic Media: Best for Beginners & Businesses

Official site: canva.com

If you hate complex tools, Canva is your friend. Go to Canva, open Magic Media, then choose Text to Video. Type your prompt and it gives you a clean clip.

  • Free plan: 50 lifetime image credits and only 5 lifetime video credits. Exports have no watermark as long as you only use free elements.
  • Best for: Marketing promos, explainer videos, Instagram Reels for business
  • Limitation: Not cinematic. It looks like stock video, and the free text-to-video clips are about 4 seconds and silent.

It's a great pick if you already use Canva for your designs.

Research note (not tested by me): Canva says free users can export video without watermarks when they use free elements, images, footage and music. Adding a Pro asset to the same design can trigger a watermark or a payment prompt, so keep your project to free elements. Canva's newer Veo 3 clip generator is on paid plans only.

4. Kling AI: Best Cinematic Quality for Free

Official site: klingai.com

Kling surprised me. It comes from a Chinese company but has an English interface, and it gives you daily free credits (66 a day when I tested). In my opinion the quality is close to Runway, which starts at about $12 a month billed annually ($15 billed monthly).

  • Free plan: 66 credits that refresh every 24 hours and do not roll over. Free exports carry a Kling watermark. Most reviews say free output is capped at 720p, and 1080p comes with a paid plan.
  • Best for: Realistic B-roll, product videos, a cinematic look
  • Limitation: The queue can be slow during peak hours, and 66 credits only covers a handful of 5-second clips a day.

I tested the prompt “a girl working on laptop in coffee shop, vertical” and the motion was super realistic.

Research note (not tested by me): Kling's free plan is for personal use only, so you need a paid plan (Standard is about $10 a month) for commercial rights and watermark-free downloads. Reviewers also report that failed generations can still use up credits.

5. InVideo AI: Best for Text-to-Video from a Script

Official site: invideo.io

You paste a full script like “Top 3 ways to make money with AI” and it builds a full video with voiceover, stock footage and captions.

  • Free plan: 10 minutes a week of AI generation and up to 4 exports a week at 720p, with an InVideo watermark and stock-media watermarks. The credits reset every Monday.
  • Best for: YouTube Shorts automation, blog-to-video
  • Limitation: Watermark on the free plan. You can crop it for Reels, but you lose some of the frame.

My Workflow to Make 3 TikToks a Day for Free

  1. Write the hook in ChatGPT: “Give me 3 scroll-stopping hooks for TikTok about AI tools.”
  2. Generate the hook clip in Pika or Kling.
  3. Assemble everything in CapCut desktop and add auto-captions.
  4. Export at 1080p, 9:16.

Total cost: $0. Total time: about 25 minutes per video.

Free Plan Gotchas That Can Cost You

Free AI video plans look generous until you try to post the result. These are the traps I'd check before you publish anything, based on each tool's pricing page and published reviews:

  • Commercial use: Kling's free plan and Pika's free plan are for personal use. If you plan to earn from your TikTok or Reels, such as brand deals or the Creator Rewards programme, you need a paid plan on those tools.
  • Credits vanish: Kling's 66 daily credits expire after 24 hours and don't roll over, so unused days are lost. Pika's 80 credits refresh monthly, and Canva's 5 video credits never refresh at all.
  • Hidden watermarks: In CapCut and Canva, one Pro-locked template or asset in a project can add a watermark to an otherwise free export. Always check the export preview.
  • Resolution caps: Free AI clips are often 480p or 720p. That's fine for a quick hook on a phone, but it can look soft next to your 1080p footage.
  • Clip length: Most free generators give you 3 to 5 seconds. Plan to stitch clips together instead of expecting a whole video.

Other Free Options Worth Knowing (Researched, Not Tested)

I haven't tested these four myself, so I'm not ranking them. This is what their pricing pages and recent reviews say, and it can change at any time:

  • Runway: The free plan gives you 125 credits once, with no monthly refresh. That's enough for a short test, and paid plans start at about $12 a month billed annually ($15 billed monthly).
  • Luma Dream Machine: It has a limited free plan that needs a Google or Apple login, with short clips of around 5 seconds. Reviewers say the exact free allowance isn't clearly stated.
  • Hailuo: It reportedly gives a one-time bundle of about 200 signup credits rather than a daily or monthly refresh.
  • PixVerse: One comparison reports roughly 50 starter credits, then 30 to 60 credits a day, and saw no visible watermark on free output. Check this on the app yourself before relying on it.

If you want to go further than the five tools above, these four are where I'd test next.

Who Should Skip These

  • InVideo AI: Skip it if you can't accept a watermark or a cropped frame.
  • Canva Magic Media: Skip it if you want cinematic, realistic footage.
  • Pika: Skip it if you want long clips without stitching.
  • CapCut: Skip it if you can't install apps and need a fully browser-based tool.

Final Verdict

Start with CapCut + Kling. CapCut for editing and captions, Kling for free cinematic AI clips. When you start making money, upgrade the tool you use most. Kling Standard at about $10 a month adds commercial rights and removes the watermark, and Runway starts at about $12 a month billed annually.

Best for: Beginners and faceless creators on a $0 budget. Not best for: Anyone who needs long, fully watermark-free AI footage with no editing step.

Which one will you try first? I recommend CapCut today.
