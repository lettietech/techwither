Meet the Human Robot: Is 1X's NEO the Future of Home Life, or Just Expensive Hype?

30-Second Quick Take

1X’s NEO humanoid robot brings advanced physical engineering to the home market for $20,000 (or $499/mo), but day-one buyers should know most impressive demos currently rely on human "Expert Mode" teleoperation rather than full autonomy.

Welcome back to TechWitHer! As AI and robotics step out of computer screens and into our living rooms, one gadget is dominating the conversation: the 1X NEO Humanoid Robot. It promises to fold your laundry, fetch your groceries, and tidy your home — but is it actually ready for that job yet?

What Is the 1X NEO Robot? A Quick Gadget Rundown

Keywords: 1X NEO robot review, humanoid robot for home, home robot 2026, NEO robot price

For years, humanoid robots stayed locked away in factories and research labs. NEO is 1X's bet that they belong in your living room instead.

  • Size & Feel: NEO weighs about 66 lbs (30 kg), wrapped in a soft knit exterior designed to feel more like a housemate than a machine — quiet enough (around 22 decibels) that it's not supposed to disrupt daily life.
  • What It's Built To Do: Fetching items, opening doors, tidying up, loading the dishwasher, folding laundry.
  • Price Tag: $20,000 outright, or $499/month through a subscription (plus a $200 refundable deposit to reserve one).

On paper, it reads like something out of a sci-fi film finally made real.

The Catch: NEO Isn't Fully Autonomous Yet

Keywords: is NEO robot autonomous, NEO robot teleoperation, NEO robot problems, humanoid robot limitations

Here's the part the marketing glosses over: when reviewers got hands-on demos, nearly every task NEO performed — fetching a water bottle, loading dishes, folding a sweater — was actually being controlled in real time by a human operator wearing a VR headset, not the robot's own AI.

1X calls this "Expert Mode." The idea is that a remote human steps in when NEO hits a task it doesn't know how to handle yet, and the robot learns from watching. It's a reasonable approach to training AI — similar to how Tesla built self-driving data from real cars on real roads. CEO Bernt Børnich has been upfront about this being temporary, calling early hiccups "robotics slop" while promising the AI will "improve drastically" as more homes come online.

But it does mean day-one buyers are paying $20,000 for a robot that currently needs a human co-pilot for most tasks — and that co-pilot can see inside your home through NEO's cameras. 1X has added safeguards (visual indicators when someone's remotely watching, no-go zones you can set in the app, and face-blurring tech), but it's a fair question whether most households are ready for that trade-off.

So, Is NEO Worth the Hype?

Keywords: should I buy NEO robot, home robot worth it, future of home robotics

If you're the kind of early adopter who wants to be part of shaping how home robotics develops — and you're comfortable with the privacy trade-offs of remote assistance — NEO is a genuinely groundbreaking first step. The hardware (five-fingered hands, gentle tendon-driven movement, a body designed to be safe around kids and pets) is impressive engineering.

If you're expecting a fully independent robot butler on day one, though, you'll want to wait a generation or two. NEO isn't there yet, and 1X isn't hiding that — you just have to read past the highlight reel to find it.

Either way, it's a clear signal: home robotics isn't science fiction anymore. It's just still figuring out how much human help it needs to grow up.
