The Flying Car Is Finally Real: Alef Model A Reaches 1,000 Flights as Tesla Joins the Race

Consumer Tech & Hardware Reviews • 2026-10-22 • By Lettie N.L.

For more than 70 years, the flying car existed mostly in movies, concept drawings, and technology promises. Now, it may finally be becoming reality.

California-based startup Alef Aeronautics recently announced that its Alef Model A flying car has completed 1,000 logged flights, marking one of the most significant milestones ever achieved by a personal flying vehicle. At the same time, Tesla revealed its own flying Roadster concept, turning what was once a niche industry into one of the most talked-about races in transportation technology.

The question is no longer whether flying cars can fly. The question is which company will get them into customer hands first.

What Is the Alef Model A Flying Car?

The Alef Model A is designed to function as both a road vehicle and an aircraft. Unlike traditional airplanes, it does not rely on fixed wings extending from the sides. Instead, the vehicle integrates multiple electric rotors within its body, allowing it to take off vertically before transitioning into forward flight.

Alef's design aims to solve one of the biggest challenges facing urban mobility: traffic congestion. Rather than sitting in traffic, the company envisions drivers lifting off and flying over it.

According to Alef, the system includes:

  • Eight hidden electric rotors
  • Vertical takeoff and landing (eVTOL) capability
  • Distributed electric propulsion
  • A rotating airframe for forward flight
  • A passenger cabin that remains level during flight
  • A ballistic parachute safety system

This makes the Alef Model A fundamentally different from most electric aircraft currently in development.

Alef Model A Specifications

While many specifications remain company-provided figures, Alef currently claims the following performance targets:

  • Driving Range: 220 miles (354 km)
  • Flight Range: 110 miles (177 km)
  • Capacity: 1 to 2 passengers
  • Road Speed: 25 mph (40 km/h)
  • Weight: Approximately 385 kg

The relatively low road speed is largely due to its classification as a Low-Speed Vehicle (LSV), allowing Alef to pursue road legality while continuing flight development.

Why the 1,000 Flight Milestone Is Important

Technology companies often announce ambitious concepts. Very few demonstrate them repeatedly.

According to Alef, the latest demonstration represented its 1,000th recorded flight, a milestone achieved after years of testing and development. The company highlighted the achievement by releasing footage showing the Model A flying over a parked Tesla Roadster and Cybertruck.

In aviation, real flights matter far more than computer renderings. That is why Alef's achievement generated so much attention across the technology and transportation industries.

Is the Alef Model A FAA Approved?

This is where many articles get the facts wrong. The short answer is: No, the Alef Model A is not fully FAA certified for unrestricted passenger operations.

However, that does not mean it lacks regulatory approval entirely. Alef previously received an FAA Special Airworthiness Certificate, allowing the company to perform research, development, and exhibition flights under specific conditions.

  • What Alef Has: Experimental flight authorization, approved test flights, research and development permissions.
  • What Alef Does Not Yet Have: Full type certification, commercial passenger approval, unrestricted public operations.

This distinction is important because no flying car company has yet achieved full large-scale passenger certification. The industry remains in a transitional stage between prototype and mass-market deployment.

Tesla Flying Car vs Alef Model A

Tesla's entry into the flying vehicle space immediately captured headlines. However, Tesla's approach appears significantly different from Alef's.

Reports surrounding Tesla's flying Roadster indicate the concept relies on cold-gas thrusters capable of producing short airborne maneuvers rather than sustained vehicle flight.

  • Alef Model A: Designed for sustained flight, electric rotor-based propulsion, focused on transportation, FAA experimental authorization.
  • Tesla Flying Roadster: Thruster-based approach, focused on extreme performance, brief airborne maneuvers, no publicly known FAA certification.

Tesla appears focused on creating the world's most exciting car. Alef appears focused on creating the world's first practical flying car.

How Much Does the Alef Model A Cost?

Innovation comes at a cost. Alef currently lists the Model A at approximately $299,999, placing it firmly within the luxury transportation category.

The company also offers reservation programs that allow interested buyers to secure a place in line for future deliveries. According to company statements, more than 3,600 reservations have already been placed for the vehicle.

The Biggest Challenges Still Ahead

Even if the technology works perfectly, significant obstacles remain:

  • Regulation: Governments must approve new forms of transportation before mass adoption becomes possible.
  • Air Traffic Management: Cities will need systems capable of safely coordinating large numbers of low-altitude aircraft.
  • Infrastructure: Charging, maintenance, storage, and takeoff infrastructure must be developed.
  • Public Trust: Consumers must feel confident enough to place their lives in autonomous and semi-autonomous flying systems.

Final Verdict: Are Flying Cars Finally Here?

The Alef Model A is not yet the mass-market flying car many people imagined. But it is one of the strongest pieces of evidence that the concept is becoming real.

Right now, Alef appears ahead in practical flying vehicle development, while Tesla remains a wildcard capable of transforming entire industries. Either way, the flying car industry has entered a new phase where the future of personal flight feels closer than ever.
